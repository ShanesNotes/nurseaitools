# Voice and design

This is the voice for the Market site, launch-week posts, and the newsletter. The look notes at the end are for the site only.

Read on 2026-10-17. Pages opened in full were [AHledger](https://ahledger.com/), the [GameMargins Forever board](https://gamemargins.com/wow-forever), and the [GameMargins addon page](https://gamemargins.com/wow-forever/addon). Search results covered [The Lazy Goldmaker](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/), a [Wowhead Classic copper ore page](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/item=2770/copper-ore), Wowhead's Classic auction house guide, a Wowhead forum thread on Auctionator and Auctioneer, [WoWAuctions](https://www.wowauctions.net/auctionHouse/classic-us/myzrael-us/alliance/copper-ore-2770), [BootyBayBroker](https://bootybaybroker.com/item/2770/copper-ore), the [TSM dashboard](https://tradeskillmaster.com/), a TSM desktop setup article, the [Countinghouse CurseForge page](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/countinghouse), and a Frostadamus vendor-flip video. ## What those pages actually do

Wowhead item pages are fact sheets. Name, quality colour, stack size, vendor sell, then player comments that name a realm and a stack price. The auction house guide is a long procedure. The useful bit for us is the comment habit. A price without a realm and a date is gossip.

The Lazy Goldmaker writes in the first person and walks one setup at a time. The tagline is "Minimal Effort, Maximum Reward". The posts that help are the ones with a number and a next click. The posts that do not help call an addon the most powerful thing in the game.

AHledger's board is the dense thing players will compare us with. Live medians, age of the scan, market named on the row. The headline "Your Bloomberg Terminal for the Azeroth economy" is the voice we will not use. Three columns of "start where you are" are the layout we will not use.

TSM's site sells a platform. "Accessible data and powerful tools" and "reach gold cap" are the sentences to avoid. Their setup article is fine because it is steps.

GameMargins is the closest useful voice. A board of crafts and vendor flips, and a line that says nothing pays right now when that is true. The addon page says what the scan does, how often the client allows it, and where the file lands.

Countinghouse's CurseForge page is a feature list, including one-press buying. Do not write a feature list that sounds like that.

Frostadamus starts a flip video by telling you to scan the auction house before you buy. That part is the whole lesson. The subscribe pitch around it is not.

WoWAuctions and BootyBayBroker put the item name, the min buyout, the average, and how old the scan is on one screen, then a table. Players stay for the number and the age.

## Voice

Plain, short, and dry. The reader is a Forever player. Lead with the item, the price, the market, and the age.

"Linen Cloth on Forever PvP Alliance US is 48c, 31 min ago." That sentence is the default voice for a price.

Front-facing copy never explains how Market works or why. No source order, no what-Market-doesn't-do, no design rationale. Say what a thing is or shows, briefly, and stop. Market should just work; the copy does not reassure, justify or lecture about where a number came from.

Market is an auction house and professions addon. Say both halves. Do not pitch it as free: every addon is free, so "free", "no paywall" and "no paid tier" are noise. Do not add a countdown.

The body of a page does not banter. No goblin joke, no treasure metaphor, no stack of exclamation marks. Jokes belong on error and dead-end lines only (see Humor).

## Humor

Humor lives on the bad moments: errors, failures, throttles, timeouts, empty or odd states, and dead ends, in the addon and on the site. When something goes wrong, poke fun at the situation, the addon or us, and try for a small smile. The players are tired of corporate copy that is too clean; a line that admits the scan wandered off reads better than "An error occurred."

Rules for an error line:

- One short line. The joke is a clause, not a paragraph.
- The action stays clear. If there is something to do next (wait, open the auction house, scan again, check the clock), say it plainly after the joke.
- Poke fun at the situation, Market or ourselves. Never at the player.
- Vary it. WoW flavor is welcome (goblins, Murlocs, the auctioneer, gnome engineering, hearthstones, Booty Bay), but not every line is a goblin joke and no single joke repeats across messages.
- No emoji, no stack of exclamation marks, and no explaining how or why Market works. The "front-facing copy never explains" rule above still holds inside a joke.
- Normal success and info copy stays plain: a finished scan, a price, the install steps, the chart, the help card, a post. The body of a page still does not banter.

Shane's examples set the register:

> The auction house goblins are busy. Try again in a bit.

The site's error line, used on the 404 page and when prices fail to load. It is Shane's own line; keep it exactly as written:

> Help. our human drove us clankers into insanity building this. get us out of here!

The Market Sync download line, on the home page's install step and in the guide. It is Shane's own line (Oct 20); keep it exactly as written, "unlock" included:

> No one wants to download separate executables, but I recommend the Sync app to unlock the potential of Market.

Account signup, if that page ever exists. It does not exist today. Do not put this line on the site until there is a real signup:

> create an account so we can collect all your data and make millions of shekels... ;)

The current error lines and their before-and-afters are listed in the humor pass (PR "copy: give error messages some personality").

## Words we use

Use these the same way every time.

- Auction house and professions addon, for what Market is. Not "auction house addon" alone.
- Auction house, not AH, in a first mention. AH is fine after that on a social post.
- Market scan, for an ask Market saw in the last hour.
- AHledger median, for their number. Always with † on a price, and a link to https://ahledger.com/ the first time a page shows one.
- Going price, for the one number Market shows.
- Price pack, for the folder the site gives you.
- Market Sync, for the program that refreshes that folder.

## Words we do not use

Unlock, elevate, seamless, powerful, robust, next-gen, gold cap as a promise, Bloomberg, terminal, dashboard, journey, guild (as in community), goblin as a brand (a goblin in an error-line joke is fine; a goblin mascot, "goblin-approved" or "our goblins" is not), to the moon. No emoji in posts or on the site, apart from the wink in the signup seed above, which stays in this guide. No "most powerful addon". No "your complete solution". No "free", "no paywall" or "no paid tier" as a pitch. No CurseForge link in a bio, a post or a newsletter; players know where addons live. No line about beating AHledger. No "never buys or sells", "own scan", "falls back" or "only when Market has nothing"; front-facing copy never explains how Market works or why.

Do not write a time we did not measure. "Install" with the three steps is enough. Do not write "in 30 seconds".

## Posts and the newsletter

One post, one fact. A price, a patch note, or one install step.

A price post names the item, the market, the number, † if it is AHledger, and the age. Link the price page. Do not tell people they will get rich.

A newsletter issue is three or four of those facts and one install or privacy note if something changed. No welcome essay. No "this week in the community".

If a market has no data, say "not open yet". Do not invent a price, and do not print 0g.

## Look

The site wears the Market design system: ink on warm paper, square 1px frames with hard offset shadows, a grotesk for statements and two monos for words and figures, and four pigments from the icon painter's palette used sparingly. The reference is typesafe.ai's launch page. The light theme (Levkas) leads; the dark theme (Carbon) follows the reader's system setting with the same roles.

`/` is a landing page for a WoW player who has not met Market: a cinnabar halftone band with a short announcement window, the hero ("Know what it's worth."), then one block per feature (prices, gold, professions, inside the game), each with a framed slot for the screenshot that will fill it. The charts follow: Market Sync's commodity charts with search, the market switcher and the watchlists, set out like a trading desk. Install and updates close the page. A link to an item or a market, followed fresh, opens on its chart; a reload keeps the place the browser restores. Static pages under `/prices/` use the same stylesheet.

`site/style.css` is the swap point. The chart reads `--leaf`, `--ink`, `--soft`, `--pencil`, `--rule`, `--rule-soft`, `--narrow`, `--candle-up`, `--candle-down`, `--listed`, and `--menu`. Those names stay. Their values are the tokens below, per theme.

Launch day is 4 Nov 2026. The site does not count down to it, and it does not print an addon version. This tree's addon and the request disagreed on the number, so a version line would be a guess.

Grounds: Levkas `--paper` `#f8f7f4`, `--leaf` `#ffffff`, `--sunk` `#efede7`, `--hover` `#f0eee8`; Carbon `#121211`, `#1b1b1a`, `#232321`, `#262523`. Hairlines are `--rule-soft` (`#dedede`, `#3a3a37`), decorative only. Every frame, button and input is a 1px `--rule`, which is the ink (14:1 or better). The focus ring is `--focus`, the lapis ink, at least 6.8:1 on paper, leaf, sunk and hover. On an inverse fill (the selected list tab) the ring takes `--on-inverse` instead, since lapis there is about 2:1. Inverted blocks (the primary button, a pressed segment, the tooltip, selected text) are `--inverse` with `--on-inverse` (15.56 and 17.00).

The four pigments each have a fill and a text ink. Cinnabar is the one accent: the halftone band, thesis labels and step numerals, and losses. Lapis is for links, the focus ring and technical lines. Gold leaf is a fill on paper (it is 2.3:1 there) and Market's gold coin letter is `--coin-gold`. Green earth is for gains. A gain or a loss also carries its sign, and colour is never the only carrier.

Item quality keeps the client's hue, set per theme until the name clears 4.7:1 on the hover row: the dark theme keeps the values this site already used, and the light theme darkens them (`--q-poor` `#626262` through `--q-heirloom` `#006a86`). The lowest pair is dark epic on the hover row, 4.80. Coin letters use `--coin-gold`, `--coin-silver` and `--coin-copper`; the letters stay visible, so a price is readable without hue. Inside the tooltip the coin letters take the tooltip's own ink.

### Type

Three faces, all self-hosted under `/fonts/` with their SIL Open Font License texts: Host Grotesk (500) for the hero, headlines, feature leads and the wordmark; Fragment Mono for words, controls and table rows; JetBrains Mono (400, 700) for figures, coins, eyebrows, column heads and window titles. Ligatures are off. Numbers are tabular, with a slashed zero.

Sizes: 10, 12, 15, 18, 22, 28, 36, 64 and 128px. The hero is clamp(56px, 9vw, 128px) at line-height 0.9 and tracking −0.04em; the item name over the chart runs 36–64px; the quote is 36px. Eyebrows, window titles and column heads are 12px uppercase at 0.05em.

A price column lines its coins up by unit. A coin that leaves out its silver or copper (`54s`, `3g`) carries their width as hidden space (`pad-c`, `pad-sc`), and a price with no † keeps the mark's place. The words a reader or a test sees do not change.

### Spacing

4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 and 96 pixels: `--space-1` through `--space-9`. The page gutter is 24px, 16px on a phone; the browser test reads the desk's 16px phone gutter. Sections are 64px apart. The reading column stays near 68 characters. Corners are square. Shadows are hard offsets only (`--shadow-hard` 2px, `--shadow-window` 3px, no blur). The only gradients are the halftone band's dot screens and their masks; the test checks both.

### Rules for this look

- † on a price is an AHledger median, linked, and named in the key. A price with no mark is a Market scan. Every table that can show † has the key directly under it, and the chart foot carries it too.
- Every page opens with a skip link to `main`. Every control shows the same 2px lapis focus ring; a control reset with `all: unset` restates it. The list tabs take one Tab stop and the arrow keys move between them.
- On a phone a price table scrolls inside its own box. The page never scrolls sideways.
- No emoji on the site. The watch control reads `[ ]` and `[x]`. Section labels are lowercase rubrics ending in a full stop; the full stop and the case are the stylesheet's, so the words in the markup stay plain.
- `/` keeps its own address until the visitor picks an item, a market, a range or a chart style, so the landing can be shared and bookmarked; from then on every choice writes a shareable address.
- One hero, one halftone band, one page h1. Feature blocks say what a player gets, briefly, and show it; their screenshot slots stay hidden until real client screenshots land; no visible placeholder. No download count, no items-tracked count, no unmeasured figure, and no `.zip` that is not a real file.
- No paid feature. Support has no donation URL until one exists.
- Humor goes on error, throttle, empty and dead-end lines, one short line each, with the next step still plain. Shane's error line stays on the 404 page and a failed load. Goblins may appear in those jokes in the scripts, never in page markup, headings or templates; `site/tests/test_style.py` checks that.
- The words in "Words we do not use" stay out of shipped copy. `site/tests/test_style.py` checks the HTML, the scripts, the stylesheet, and the sitegen templates. The windows look like terminals; the words never call them that.
- Lightweight Charts stays, with its attribution, until a measured problem says otherwise.
